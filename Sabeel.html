Six first steps

New Muslim Roadmap

If you've recently embraced Islam — or are considering it — this is the short list. Islam is learned gradually, not overnight: "Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity" (Qur'an 2:286). Each step below cites its source.

1

Understand your Shahada

The declaration — "Ash-hadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasulullah" — means: I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah. Saying it sincerely is how a person enters Islam; no ceremony or witness is required.

أَنَّ اللَّهَ حَرَّمَ النَّارَ عَلَىٰ مَنْ قَالَ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ يَبْتَغِي بِذَلِكَ وَجْهَ اللَّهِ

"Allah has forbidden the Fire for the one who says 'There is no god but Allah' — yabtaghī bidhālika wajhallāh (يَبْتَغِي بِذَلِكَ وَجْهَ اللَّهِ, seeking by it the Face of Allah, i.e. sincerely for Him alone, not for show or people's praise)."

Sahih al-Bukhari 425 Sahih

The first half negates all worship for anything besides Allah; the second half affirms Prophet Muhammad ﷺ as His final messenger (Qur'an 33:40). Read the full breakdown under Core Topics → The Shahada.

2

Learn wudhu and salah — and guard them

Prayer is the most repeated obligation in a Muslim's day, so it comes first in practice. Learn it slowly and correctly rather than quickly and roughly.

أَحَبُّ الْأَعْمَالِ إِلَى اللَّهِ أَدْوَمُهَا وَإِنْ قَلَّ

"The deeds most beloved to Allah are those done consistently — adwamuha (أَدْوَمُهَا, the most continuous/persistent) — even if they are small."

Sahih al-Bukhari 6464, Sahih Muslim 783 Sahih

Use the Prayer Times and Qibla Finder tools on this site to know when and which direction to pray.

3

Learn what you believe

The six articles of Iman (faith), taught by the angel Gabriel himself in the Hadith of Jibril: belief in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and divine decree (Sahih Muslim 8). Each has a card under Core Topics.

"…and He is with you wherever you are."

Qur'an 57:4
4

Connect every Friday (for men) and with your community

The Friday congregational prayer (Jumu'ah) replaces the Dhuhr prayer on Friday for men who attend; women may pray at home or attend, and it is not obligatory on them. It is the weekly anchor of the Muslim community.

"O you who believe! When the call is proclaimed for prayer on the day of Friday, hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade."

Qur'an 62:9
5

Keep learning, a little every day

A daily habit of reading Qur'an — even a few verses with translation — and learning one new thing at a time will do more than bursts of enthusiasm.

"Whoever treads a path in search of knowledge, Allah will make easy for him a path to Paradise."

Sahih Muslim 2699 Sahih

"The best among you are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it."

Sahih al-Bukhari 5027 Sahih
6

Keep a few simple daily duas

اللَّهُمَّ رَبَّ هَذِهِ الدَّعْوَةِ التَّامَّةِ وَالصَّلَاةِ الْقَائِمَةِ

After the call to prayer: "O Allah, Lord of this perfect call and the prayer to be offered, grant Muhammad ﷺ the intercession and favor."

Sahih al-Bukhari 614 Sahih

Before eating: the Prophet ﷺ said to the young companion Umar ibn Abi Salamah: "Sammillāh, wa kul bi-yamīnik" — "Mention Allah's name (سَمِّ اللَّهَ) and eat with your right hand (وَكُلْ بِيَمِينِكَ)..."

Sahih Muslim 2022 Sahih
رَبَّنَا آتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً وَقِنَا عَذَابَ النَّارِ

"Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire."

Qur'an 2:201
Note: New Muslims are excused for what they don't yet know, and Allah does not punish anyone before the message reaches them clearly (Qur'an 17:15). Focus on the essentials first — prayer, honesty, and kindness — and let knowledge grow steadily. When in doubt on a ruling, ask a qualified local scholar rather than relying on any app, including this one.

Step by step

Wudhu & Salah

How to perform ablution and prayer, step by step, based on how the Prophet ﷺ described and demonstrated it.

1Wudhu (Ablution)

لَا يَقْبَلُ اللَّهُ صَلَاةَ أَحَدِكُمْ إِذَا أَحْدَثَ حَتَّىٰ يَتَوَضَّأَ

"Allah does not accept the prayer of anyone among you if he commits Hadath (أَحْدَثَ — enters a state of minor impurity, e.g. by using the toilet or passing wind) until he performs Wudhu."

Prophet Muhammad ﷺ — Sahih al-Bukhari 135 Sahih

Wudhu begins with the intention in the heart to purify oneself for prayer. The intention is made in the heart alone: the Prophet ﷺ never uttered the intention aloud — not for wudhu and not for prayer — even though he taught every other detail of worship in words. The scholars, such as Shaykh Ibn Baz (may Allah have mercy on him, fatwa: Hukm at-Talfudh bil-Niyyah), ruled that pronouncing the intention is therefore not from the Sunnah at all, but an innovation (bid'ah) with no basis in his ﷺ practice or that of his companions. The proof that intention belongs to the heart is the foundation hadith of the religion:

إِنَّمَا الْأَعْمَالُ بِالنِّيَّاتِ… وَكُلُّ امْرِئٍ مَا نَوَى

"Deeds are but by intentions (إِنَّمَا الْأَعْمَالُ بِالنِّيَّاتِ — every act is judged by the intention behind it), and every person will have only what he intended."

Sahih al-Bukhari 1, Sahih Muslim 1907 — narrated by Umar ibn al-Khattab Sahih

Begin by saying "Bismillah" (in the name of Allah).

"There is no wudhu (complete, perfect wudhu) for one who does not mention the name of Allah over it."

Sunan Abu Dawud 101 Hasan — graded hasan by al-Tirmidhi; al-Albani graded it sahih. All scholars agree mentioning Allah's name is at least strongly recommended

Wash both hands up to the wrists, three times, making sure water reaches between the fingers.

Take water into the mouth and swish it, then spit it out. Sniff water gently into the nostrils and blow it out. Traditionally done together, three times each.

From hairline to chin, ear to ear, three times.

Right arm first, then left, including the elbow itself, three times each.

With wet hands, wipe from the front of the head to the back and then back to the front, once. Then wipe the ears, inside and out, with the same water.

Right foot first, then left, including the ankles, making sure water reaches between the toes, three times each.

مَنْ تَوَضَّأَ فَأَحْسَنَ الْوُضُوءَ ثُمَّ شَهِدَ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ…

"Whoever performs wudhu well (fa-ahsana al-wudū' — أَحْسَنَ الْوُضُوءَ, doing it thoroughly and correctly), then testifies: 'I bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, without partner, and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger' — the eight gates of Paradise are opened for him, he may enter through whichever he wishes."

Sahih Muslim 234 Sahih
Note: The four legal schools agree on this general sequence, drawn primarily from Qur'an 5:6 and the Prophet's ﷺ demonstrated practice, with minor differences on which steps are obligatory (fard) versus recommended (sunnah) — for example, the Hanafi school treats rinsing the mouth and nose as recommended rather than obligatory, while the Shafi'i school treats wiping a smaller portion of the head as sufficient. What's presented above is the complete, agreed-upon full practice.

✦Am I still pure? — the complete picture

What breaks wudhu
  • Anything exiting the two private passages — urine, stool, wind: "Allah does not accept the prayer of anyone who passes wind until he performs wudhu" (Sahih al-Bukhari 137).
  • Deep sleep — one who rises from sleep should wash before touching vessels (Sahih al-Bukhari 188). Light dozing while seated does not break wudhu.
  • Loss of consciousness or intoxication.
  • Camel meat, per the hadith (Sahih Muslim 360) — the Hanafis treat it as breaking wudhu; the other schools do not.
When ghusl (full bath) is required
  • After sexual activity or emission of seminal fluid: "If you are in a state of janabah, purify yourselves" (Qur'an 4:43).
  • After menstruation or post-natal bleeding ends — the Prophet ﷺ commanded women in that state to perform ghusl and pray (Sahih al-Bukhari 326).
  • Recommended, not required: Friday (Sahih al-Bukhari 876), entering ihram, and the Eid prayers.
Tayammum — purity without water
  • When no water is found, or using it would harm you: "…then take for yourselves clean earth and wipe your faces and hands with it" (Qur'an 4:43, 5:6).
  • How: strike clean earth once with both palms, blow off the dust, wipe the face, then wipe the hands (Sahih al-Bukhari 347, from Ali).
  • "The earth was made a place of prayer and purification for me" (Sahih al-Bukhari 335) — it is a mercy, not a burden (Qur'an 5:6).

Where schools differ (camel meat, skin contact with a woman), the difference is stated rather than hidden — follow a qualified teacher's guidance on those details.

2How to Pray (Salah)

One unit (rak'ah) of prayer, in sequence. A prayer is 2, 3, or 4 such units depending on which of the five it is.

Stand facing the direction of the Kaaba. The intention (niyyah) is made in the heart alone — knowing which prayer you are about to perform. It is never pronounced aloud: the Prophet ﷺ never did, and the scholars rule that verbalizing it is an innovation (bid'ah) with no basis in the Sunnah.

Raise both hands to shoulder or ear level and say "Allahu Akbar" (God is greatest). This is called Takbirat al-Ihram — it formally begins the prayer, after which speaking or eating is not permitted until it ends.

Hands placed on the chest (right over left, per the stronger hadith evidence, though schools differ on exact placement). Recite Surah al-Fatihah — obligatory in every rak'ah — followed by another portion of the Qur'an in the first two rak'ahs.

لَا صَلَاةَ لِمَنْ لَمْ يَقْرَأْ بِفَاتِحَةِ الْكِتَابِ

"There is no prayer for the one who does not recite the Opening of the Book (فَاتِحَةِ الْكِتَابِ — Surah al-Fatihah)."

Sahih al-Bukhari 756, Sahih Muslim 394 Sahih

Say "Allahu Akbar," bow with back flat and hands on knees, and say "Subhana Rabbiyal Adheem" (Glory be to my Lord the Magnificent) at least three times.

Stand fully upright, saying "Sami' Allahu liman hamidah" (Allah hears whoever praises Him), then "Rabbana wa lakal hamd" (Our Lord, to You is all praise).

Say "Allahu Akbar" and go down on forehead, nose, both palms, both knees, and toes touching the ground. Say "Subhana Rabbiyal A'la" (Glory be to my Lord the Most High) at least three times. This posture is described as the point of closest nearness to God.

أَقْرَبُ مَا يَكُونُ الْعَبْدُ مِنْ رَبِّهِ وَهُوَ سَاجِدٌ فَأَكْثِرُوا الدُّعَاءَ

"The closest a servant is to his Lord is while he is prostrating (سَاجِدٌ), so increase supplication then."

Sahih Muslim 482 Sahih

Rise to a brief sitting position saying "Allahu Akbar," then go into a second prostration identical to the first.

This completes one rak'ah. Stand again for the next, repeating from the recitation step, until the prayer's total rak'ah count is complete. The counts for the five daily prayers — agreed upon unanimously:

Fajr
2
Dhuhr
4
Asr
4
Maghrib
3
Isha
4

Beyond the five, there is Witr — an odd-numbered prayer (commonly 3 rak'ahs) offered after Isha: "Allah has added for you a prayer — Witr — so pray it between Isha and dawn" (Sunan Abu Dawud 1419 Sahih, graded sahih by al-Albani). The Hanafi school counts Witr as wajib (obligatory); the other three schools as a strongly emphasized sunnah.

In the last rak'ah, sit and recite the Tashahhud (a testimony of faith and greeting), followed by sending blessings on the Prophet ﷺ. The prayer closes by turning the head to the right, then the left, saying "Assalamu alaikum wa rahmatullah" (peace and mercy of God be upon you) each time — the Taslim.

The exact words of the final sitting, taught by the Prophet ﷺ himself:

التَّحِيَّاتُ لِلَّهِ وَالصَّلَوَاتُ وَالطَّيِّبَاتُ، السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكَ أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ وَبَرَكَاتُهُ، السَّلَامُ عَلَيْنَا وَعَلَىٰ عِبَادِ اللَّهِ الصَّالِحِينَ، أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ

"All greetings of humility, all prayers, and all goodness are for Allah. Peace be upon you, O Prophet, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings. Peace be upon us and upon the righteous servants of Allah. I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah."

Sahih al-Bukhari 831 — taught directly by the Prophet ﷺ to Ibn Mas'ud Sahih

Then, before the closing salam, send blessings on the Prophet ﷺ:

اللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَىٰ مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَىٰ آلِ مُحَمَّدٍ كَمَا صَلَّيْتَ عَلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَعَلَىٰ آلِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ، إِنَّكَ حَمِيدٌ مَجِيدٌ

"O Allah, send prayers upon Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You sent prayers upon Ibrahim and the family of Ibrahim. You are indeed Praiseworthy, Most Honorable."

Sahih al-Bukhari 3370 Sahih

The Prophet ﷺ taught a brief set of glorifications after each obligatory prayer:

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ ﴿٣٣﴾ وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ ﴿٣٣﴾ وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ ﴿٣٣﴾… لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، لَهُ الْمُلْكُ وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ

Say "SubhanAllah" (Glory be to Allah) 33 times, "Alhamdulillah" (All praise is Allah's) 33 times, and "Allahu Akbar" (Allah is greatest) 33 times — then to complete one hundred: "There is no god but Allah alone, without partner; His is the dominion and His is the praise, and He is over all things competent."

Sahih Muslim 595 Sahih

Also, reciting Ayat al-Kursi (Qur'an 2:255) after each prayer is strongly rewarded:

"Whoever recites Ayat al-Kursi after every obligatory prayer, nothing stands between him and entering Paradise except death."

Sunan an-Nasa'i 100 — graded sahih by al-Albani Sahih

The adhan is called aloud at the time of each prayer. Its wording, in order:

  • "Allahu Akbar" (God is greatest) — ×4
  • "Ash-hadu an la ilaha illallah" (I bear witness there is no god but Allah) — ×2
  • "Ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasulullah" (I bear witness Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah) — ×2
  • "Hayya 'ala as-salah" (Come to prayer) — ×2
  • "Hayya 'ala al-falah" (Come to success) — ×2
  • In Fajr only: "As-salatu khayrun min an-nawm" (Prayer is better than sleep) — ×2
  • "Allahu Akbar" — ×2, then "La ilaha illallah" (There is no god but Allah) — ×1

The wording has remained fixed since the Prophet ﷺ established it, and is the same worldwide.

On Friday, the Dhuhr prayer is replaced by Jumu'ah for men who attend the congregation: a sermon (khutbah) followed by a short 2-rak'ah prayer, prayed aloud together. Women may attend or pray Dhuhr at home instead — it is not obligatory on them, travelers, or the sick. Women's attendance is permitted, not forbidden: "If the wife of any one of you asks permission to go to the mosque, do not forbid her" (Sahih al-Bukhari 865 Sahih).

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا نُودِيَ لِلصَّلَاةِ مِن يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ فَاسْعَوْا إِلَىٰ ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ وَذَرُوا الْبَيْعَ

"O you who believe! When the call is proclaimed for prayer on the day of Friday, hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave off trade. That is better for you, if you only knew."

Qur'an 62:9

"The best day on which the sun has risen is Friday: on it Adam was created, on it he entered Paradise, and on it he was expelled from it."

Sahih Muslim 854 Sahih

It is recommended to perform ghusl (a full bath) before attending, wear one's best clothes, and arrive early.

"Ghusl on Friday is a duty for every adult Muslim."

Sahih al-Bukhari 876 Sahih
Note: Small differences in hand placement, exact wording of some supplications, and posture details exist between the four schools of fiqh — all traced to authentic but slightly varying hadith reports of the Prophet ﷺ on different occasions. None of these differences affect the prayer's validity across schools.

Core belief

Tawheed — The Oneness of God

The central organizing concept of Islamic belief. Classical scholars, particularly from the Athari/Salafi and Ash'ari-Maturidi traditions, generally break it into these categories to teach it clearly — the division is a scholarly teaching tool, not a Qur'anic verse itself.

قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ﴿١﴾ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ ﴿٢﴾ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ﴿٣﴾ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ ﴿٤﴾

"Say: He is Allah, [who is] One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born. Nor is there to Him any equivalent."

Qur'an 112 (Surah al-Ikhlas) — the Prophet ﷺ said it equals one third of the Qur'an in meaning (Sahih al-Bukhari 5013 Sahih)

Affirming that God alone is the Creator, Sustainer, and Controller of all existence — nothing happens without His will.

اللَّهُ خَالِقُ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ

"Allah is the Creator of all things."

Qur'an 39:62

The Qur'an notes that even the pagans the Prophet ﷺ addressed generally accepted this part — they agreed God created the heavens and earth (Qur'an 29:61) — yet still fell into shirk elsewhere. Believing God is the Creator, on its own, was considered insufficient for true tawheed.

Directing all acts of worship — prayer, supplication, sacrifice, vows, seeking help in matters beyond human power — to God alone, with no intermediary.

وَقَضَىٰ رَبُّكَ أَلَّا تَعْبُدُوا إِلَّا إِيَّاهُ

"And your Lord has decreed that you worship none but Him."

Qur'an 17:23

Scholars describe this as the category most prophets were specifically sent to correct — since many peoples affirmed a creator God in the abstract, but still worshiped other beings or objects alongside Him.

Affirming the names and attributes God describes Himself with in the Qur'an and authentic Sunnah, without denying them, distorting their meaning, asking "how," or comparing them to creation.

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ ۖ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ

"There is nothing like unto Him, and He is the Hearing, the Seeing."

Qur'an 42:11
Note: This particular category has historically been the most debated between theological schools — Athari scholars (e.g. Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Ibn Taymiyyah) affirm attributes like God's "hand" or "rising over the Throne" as real but unlike creation, without asking how; Ash'ari and Maturidi theologians (e.g. al-Ghazali, later Hanafi/Shafi'i mainstream) tend to interpret some such terms metaphorically to avoid any implication of resemblance to creation. Both camps affirm God's absolute oneness and uniqueness — the disagreement is about method of handling specific texts, not about tawheed itself.

Shirk means associating partners with God — in His lordship, His right to worship, or His attributes. It is described in the Qur'an as the one sin God does not forgive if a person dies upon it unrepentant.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَغْفِرُ أَن يُشْرَكَ بِهِ وَيَغْفِرُ مَا دُونَ ذَٰلِكَ لِمَن يَشَاءُ

"Indeed, Allah does not forgive association with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills."

Qur'an 4:48

Scholars distinguish Shirk Akbar (major shirk — e.g. worshiping an idol, a saint, or oneself in place of God) which nullifies faith, from Shirk Asghar (minor shirk — e.g. showing off in worship, riya') which is a serious sin but does not remove someone from Islam.

How rulings are derived

Fiqh Basics

Fiqh is the scholarly discipline of deriving practical rulings from the Qur'an and Sunnah. Where the two primary sources are the revealed texts themselves, fiqh is the human, reasoned effort to apply them — which is why qualified scholars can differ on secondary questions.

Scholars generally rank the sources of Islamic law in this order of authority:

  • Qur'an — the primary, unquestionable source.
  • Sunnah — the Prophet's ﷺ authenticated example, explaining and detailing the Qur'an.
  • Ijma' — scholarly consensus, where all qualified scholars of an era agreed on a ruling.
  • Qiyas — analogical reasoning, extending a known ruling to a new situation that shares its underlying reason ('illah).

Some schools also weigh additional principles such as maslahah (public benefit) or 'urf (established custom), within bounds set by the primary texts.

  • Fard / Wajib — obligatory. Reward for doing it, sin for abandoning it. (e.g. the five daily prayers)
  • Mustahabb / Sunnah — recommended. Reward for doing it, no sin for leaving it. (e.g. voluntary prayers)
  • Mubah — neutral / permissible. No reward or sin either way. (e.g. choice of permissible food)
  • Makruh — disliked. No sin, but discouraged. (e.g. excessive worldly talk in the mosque)
  • Haram — forbidden. Sin for doing it. (e.g. consuming intoxicants)

Four schools of fiqh became the major surviving traditions within Sunni Islam, each founded by an early scholar and developed by generations of students after them:

  • Hanafi — founded on the teachings of Imam Abu Hanifah (d. 767 CE), historically centered in Iraq; today widespread across Turkey, the Balkans, Central and South Asia.
  • Maliki — founded on the teachings of Imam Malik ibn Anas (d. 795 CE) in Medina; historically widespread across North and West Africa.
  • Shafi'i — founded on the teachings of Imam al-Shafi'i (d. 820 CE); widespread in East Africa, the Levant, and Southeast Asia.
  • Hanbali — founded on the teachings of Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal (d. 855 CE); the predominant school in Saudi Arabia today.
Note: These schools agree on the large majority of Islamic practice — the pillars, the prohibition of major sins, core beliefs — and differ mainly on secondary procedural details, each grounded in a different but scholarly-defensible reading of the same body of textual evidence. Following any one of them is considered valid. This tool presents the Sunni schools, which the large majority of Muslims follow; other long-established Muslim communities — such as Shia (Ja'fari) and Ibadi Muslims — have their own schools with many shared rulings and some differing details not covered here.

Ijtihad is the effort a qualified scholar (mujtahid) makes to derive a ruling on a matter not explicitly and unambiguously addressed in the primary texts, using the established sources and methodology.

A widely cited report describes the Prophet ﷺ asking Mu'adh ibn Jabal, before sending him to Yemen, how he would judge matters — Mu'adh answered: by the Qur'an, then the Sunnah, then his own reasoning (ijtihad) if he found nothing in either, and the Prophet ﷺ approved.

Sunan Abu Dawud 3592 Da'if — hadith critics including al-Albani judge its chain weak (unidentified narrators); scholars of usul al-fiqh still cite it widely as illustrating an established methodology, but it should not be leaned on as a strong proof by itself

Where ijtihad on the same question produces different conclusions between sincere, qualified scholars, classical scholarship generally holds that the mujtahid who reaches the correct conclusion is rewarded twice, and the one who reasoned carefully but erred is still rewarded once for the effort.

"If a judge gives a verdict, having tried his best to decide correctly, and he is right, then he will have a double reward. If he gives a verdict after having tried his best but he is wrong, then he will have a single reward."

Sahih al-Bukhari 7352 Sahih

Meat is halal (permissible) when the animal is of a permitted species, slaughtered by a Muslim (or in some scholarly views, a Jew or Christian per Qur'an 5:5), with a swift cut to the throat while invoking God's name.

  • Pork and its by-products are explicitly forbidden (Qur'an 5:3).
  • Alcohol and intoxicants are forbidden (Qur'an 5:90).
  • Animals that died by other means (strangled, beaten, fallen, gored, or partly eaten by a predator before being properly slaughtered) are forbidden (Qur'an 5:3), unless they died naturally underwater, like fish, which are generally permitted.
Ninth month

Ramadan & Fasting

The ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar, and the month in which fasting (Sawm) — the fourth pillar of Islam — is obligatory.

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ

"O you who believe, fasting has been decreed upon you as it was decreed upon those before you, that you may attain God-consciousness (taqwa)."

Qur'an 2:183

From dawn (Fajr) to sunset (Maghrib), a fasting person abstains from:

  • Eating and drinking, including water.
  • Marital relations.
  • Intentional vomiting.

Who fasts: sane, healthy, adult Muslims. Exemptions and accommodations exist for the sick, travelers, the elderly, pregnant or nursing women, and menstruating women — most of these make up missed days later, or, where make-up isn't reasonably possible, feed a poor person for each missed day instead (Qur'an 2:184–185).

Suhoor is the pre-dawn meal, eaten before Fajr. The Prophet ﷺ encouraged it:

"Eat suhoor, for in suhoor there is blessing."

Sahih al-Bukhari 1923, Sahih Muslim 1095 Sahih

Iftar is breaking the fast at sunset. The Prophet ﷺ recommended breaking it promptly rather than delaying, traditionally with dates and water.

"The people will remain upon good as long as they hasten to break the fast."

Sahih al-Bukhari 1957, Sahih Muslim 1098 Sahih

An extended voluntary night prayer performed specifically in Ramadan, often with lengthy Qur'an recitation, commonly completing the whole Qur'an by the month's end in many mosques.

"Whoever stands [in prayer] during Ramadan out of faith and seeking reward, his previous sins will be forgiven."

Sahih al-Bukhari 37, Sahih Muslim 759 Sahih

A night in the last ten days of Ramadan — most scholars point to the odd-numbered nights, particularly the 27th — described in the Qur'an as better than a thousand months.

لَيْلَةُ الْقَدْرِ خَيْرٌ مِّنْ أَلْفِ شَهْرٍ

"The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months."

Qur'an 97:3

"Seek Laylat al-Qadr in the odd nights of the last ten nights of Ramadan."

Sahih al-Bukhari 2017 Sahih

This is the night traditionally understood as when the Qur'an's revelation first began. Many Muslims spend these nights in the mosque in i'tikaf (seclusion for worship), following the Prophet's ﷺ practice (Sahih al-Bukhari 2026).

A small obligatory charity of food (or its cash equivalent, per many contemporary scholars) given by or on behalf of every Muslim before the Eid prayer, distinct from the annual Zakat on wealth.

The Prophet ﷺ made Zakat al-Fitr obligatory as purification for the fasting person from idle or indecent talk, and as food for the needy.

Sunan Abu Dawud 1609 Hasan

The festival marking the end of Ramadan, on the first day of the following month (Shawwal). Marked by a special congregational morning prayer, and it is forbidden to fast on this day.

"No fasting is permissible on two days: the day of al-Fitr and the day of al-Adha."

Sahih al-Bukhari 1990 Sahih

The Eid prayer itself is two rak'ahs, with no adhan or iqamah before it, followed by a sermon: "The Prophet ﷺ prayed two rak'ahs on the day of al-Fitr, without praying anything before or after them" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1956 Sahih).

Fifth pillar

Hajj & Umrah

The pilgrimage to Mecca — obligatory once in a lifetime on every Muslim who has the physical and financial ability, performed in the month of Dhul-Hijjah. Umrah is the "lesser pilgrimage," which can be performed at any time of year.

وَأَتِمُّوا الْحَجَّ وَالْعُمْرَةَ لِلَّهِ

"And complete the Hajj and Umrah for Allah…"

Qur'an 2:196

The Qur'an makes the obligation conditional on ability:

وَلِلَّهِ عَلَى النَّاسِ حِجُّ الْبَيْتِ مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا

"And due to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House — for whoever is able to find a way there."

Qur'an 3:97

Scholars understand "able" to mean physically capable and financially able to make the journey without incurring hardship or debt.

"Whoever performs Hajj and does not commit obscenity or transgression, returns [free of sin] as the day his mother bore him."

Sahih al-Bukhari 1521 Sahih

"Umrah to Umrah is expiation for whatever [sins] come between them, and the accepted Hajj has no reward but Paradise."

Sahih al-Bukhari 1773 Sahih
  • Ihram — entering a state of consecration at the designated boundary (miqat): simple white unstitched cloth for men, modest plain dress for women, with the intention declared aloud ("Labbayk Allahumma labbayk" — here I am, O Allah). Intimacy, quarreling, cutting hair or nails, and perfume become forbidden during it (Qur'an 2:197).
  • Tawaf — circling the Kaaba seven times counter-clockwise.
  • Sa'i — walking seven times between the hills of Safa and Marwah, retracing Hajar's search for water (Qur'an 2:158).
  • Day of Arafah (9th Dhul-Hijjah) — standing in worship on the plain of Arafah; the heart of Hajj. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Hajj is Arafah" (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 889, graded sahih by al-Albani).
  • Muzdalifah & Mina — the night's rest and the stoning of the pillars symbolizing rejection of Satan.
  • Eid al-Adha & Udhiyah — the sacrifice of an animal commemorating Ibrahim's devotion (Qur'an 22:34, 37:102–107).
  • Tawaf al-Ifadah — a final circling of the Kaaba, after which the Ihram restrictions lift.

The tenth of Dhul-Hijjah is Eid al-Adha, the "festival of sacrifice," the greatest day of the year. Fasting is forbidden on it, as on Eid al-Fitr (Sahih al-Bukhari 1990).

The first ten days of Dhul-Hijjah are the most beloved days of the year for good deeds:

"There are no days during which righteous deeds are more beloved to Allah than these ten days."

Sahih al-Bukhari 969 Sahih

Muslims who are not on Hajj often fast the first nine days — especially the Day of Arafah, which the Prophet ﷺ said expiates the sins of the previous year and the coming year (Sahih Muslim 1162) — and offer the Udhiyah sacrifice if able.

Hajj is obligatory once in a lifetime for the able, performed only in Dhul-Hijjah, and includes the Day of Arafah. Umrah is a shorter visit (Ihram, Tawaf, Sa'i, and cutting the hair) valid at any time of year; it is highly recommended — many scholars hold it obligatory once in a lifetime if able, others hold it a strong sunnah.

"Umrah to Umrah is expiation for whatever [sins] come between them…"

Sahih al-Bukhari 1773 Sahih

The Kaaba itself was (re)built by Ibrahim and Isma'il (Qur'an 2:127), and Muslims do not worship it — it is simply the unified direction of prayer, as Umar famously said of the Black Stone: "I know that you are a stone that neither benefits nor harms" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1597).
